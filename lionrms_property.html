THE LEGAL LANDSCAPE

"I Didn't Know" Is Not a Defence When a Council Inspector Calls

The obligations on landlords have increased significantly since the Housing Act 2004 and subsequent updates. Here is what applies to your properties.

🔥

RR(FS)O 2005 — Fire Risk Assessment

Blocks of flats, HMOs, and any property where the landlord controls common areas must have a written fire risk assessment carried out by a competent person. The assessment must be reviewed whenever there is reason to suspect it is no longer valid.

Applies to: HMOs · Blocks of flats · Mixed-use
Penalty: unlimited fine · imprisonment up to 2 years
💧

HSE L8 ACoP — Legionella

All landlords have a duty under the HSWA 1974 and COSHH Regulations to assess and control the risk of Legionella in their properties. For rental properties with hot and cold water systems, a written Legionella risk assessment is required.

Applies to: All rental properties
Penalty: unlimited fine · prosecution · civil liability
🏠

Housing Act 2004 — HMO Licensing

HMOs with 5 or more occupants in 2+ households require mandatory HMO licensing. The licence application requires evidence of satisfactory fire safety arrangements, including fire risk assessment and installation of appropriate detection systems.

Applies to: HMOs — 5+ occupants, 2+ households
Penalty: up to £30,000 fine · rent repayment order
📋

HHSRS — Housing Health and Safety

The Housing Health and Safety Rating System allows councils to inspect and rate hazards in rented properties. Fire and Legionella are Category 1 hazards — the most serious. A Category 1 hazard triggers mandatory enforcement action.

Applies to: All residential rental properties
Penalty: improvement notice · prohibition order
⚡

Electrical Safety Standards 2020

Landlords must have electrical installations inspected and tested every 5 years by a qualified engineer (EICR). The report must be provided to tenants within 28 days and to the local authority within 7 days of request.

Applies to: All private rented sector properties
Penalty: up to £30,000 fine per property
🏢

Building Safety Act 2022 — Higher Risk

Buildings over 18 metres or 7 storeys with residential units now face the Building Safety Regime — requiring a designated Principal Accountable Person, safety case report, and resident engagement strategy. Significant for portfolio landlords.

Applies to: Buildings 18m+ / 7+ storeys
Penalty: unlimited fine · criminal liability
