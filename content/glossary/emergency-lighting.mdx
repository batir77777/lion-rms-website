A fire that takes out the lighting circuit leaves an escape route no less necessary and considerably harder to use. Emergency lighting exists for exactly that case: it comes on automatically when the normal supply fails, and it stays on long enough for the building to be evacuated.

## What it has to cover

Coverage follows the escape route rather than the floor plan — the route itself, changes of direction, stairs and level changes, exit doors, final exits, and the locations of fire alarm call points and fire-fighting equipment. Exit signage forms part of the same provision and needs to be legible under emergency conditions, not only under normal lighting.

## Duration and testing

Systems are specified to maintain illumination for a defined duration after failure, and that duration is the whole point of the specification — a luminaire that operates for a few seconds satisfies nothing. Routine testing therefore checks not only that fittings illuminate but that they sustain it: brief monthly function tests, and a full-duration test annually, both recorded.

Failed fittings are common and easy to miss, because nothing about a building's normal appearance reveals them. The test record is the only practical evidence that the provision still works.
