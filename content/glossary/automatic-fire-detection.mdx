Automatic fire detection removes the dependency on a person noticing a fire and acting. Detectors sense heat, smoke or flame, the control panel processes the signal, and the sounders raise the alarm — all without human intervention. Manual call points remain part of the same system, so a person who spots a fire first can still trigger it directly.

## Category grading

Systems are specified by category, and the category reflects what the system is there to protect. Categories in the L range are designed to protect life, from full coverage down to detection limited to escape routes; categories in the P range are designed to protect property. Which category a building needs follows from its fire strategy, its occupancy and the risks identified in its fire risk assessment — it is not a free choice made at installation.

## Why the grading matters in practice

A system installed to the wrong category can be fully functional and still leave a building inadequately protected, because coverage was never specified where the risk actually sits. That is a common finding on older installations where the building's use has changed since the system went in but the detection layout never followed.

## Testing and maintenance

Detection is only as good as its upkeep: weekly user testing of a call point in rotation, and periodic servicing by a competent contractor. Records of both matter — a system in good order with no evidence of testing is difficult to defend when it is examined after an incident.
