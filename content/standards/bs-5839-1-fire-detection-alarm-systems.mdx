## What it is for

BS 5839-1 is the code of practice for fire detection and fire alarm systems in non-domestic buildings. It covers the full life of a system: how it should be designed, how it should be installed, how it should be commissioned and handed over, and how it should be maintained thereafter.

It is the origin of the system category notation — the L and M and P designations — that appears on almost every fire alarm specification, certificate and service report in the country, and of the routine testing and servicing regime that duty holders are expected to keep records of.

## Scope and typical application

Non-domestic premises: workplaces, public buildings, education, healthcare, retail, industrial and warehouse buildings, and the non-residential parts of mixed-use developments. Systems in dwellings are covered by other parts of the BS 5839 series, and evacuation alert systems for use by fire and rescue services in blocks of flats are dealt with separately again.

In fire risk assessment work the standard turns up in three recurring ways. Establishing what category of system the building actually has, and whether that category is appropriate to the risk and the evacuation strategy. Checking whether the routine testing and servicing regime has been kept up and evidenced. And judging whether a system that was appropriate when installed still matches a building whose layout, use or occupancy has since changed.

## How it relates to legal duty

A British Standard is not law. The duty is to have adequate general fire precautions and a suitable and sufficient fire risk assessment — in England and Wales, under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 — and neither the Order nor the Building Regulations specifies a system category.

What BS 5839-1 provides is the benchmark against which "adequate" detection and warning is judged in practice. Departures from it are permissible and sometimes appropriate, but the standard's own expectation is that any variation is recorded rather than left implicit, and an unrecorded departure is very difficult to defend after an incident.

## What this means in practice

The most common finding in this area is not a missing system but a mismatched one: a category installed for a building that has since been subdivided, changed use, or acquired sleeping accommodation. The second most common is a maintenance record that stops. Neither shows up unless somebody checks the system against the building as it is now rather than as it was when the certificate was issued.

## Status and currency

BS 5839-1:2025 was published on 30 April 2025 and is current. It supersedes BS 5839-1:2017, which is withdrawn. No amendments or corrigenda have been identified against the 2025 edition.

One caution worth recording: secondary sources referring to a "BS 5839-1:2026" are in circulation. No such edition is recorded by BSI or by the NBS publication index, and the current designation is BS 5839-1:2025.
