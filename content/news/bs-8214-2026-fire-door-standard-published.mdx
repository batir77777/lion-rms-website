BSI published BS 8214:2026 on 20 March 2026. It supersedes BS 8214:2016 and now
carries the title *Fire-resisting and smoke control doors — Practical
considerations concerning specification, design and performance in use — Code of
practice*.

## What has changed at the level of scope

The title itself is the first thing to notice. The 2016 edition addressed
fire-resisting doorsets; the 2026 edition brings smoke control doors explicitly
into scope. Smoke, not flame, is what makes an escape route unusable first, and a
code of practice that treats smoke control as a named part of door performance
rather than a consequence of fire resistance is a more honest reflection of what
a <GlossaryLink slug="fire-door">fire door</GlossaryLink> is doing in a building.

## What it means for inspection work

BS 8214 is a code of practice, not a regulation. It does not create a duty. What
it does is set out what good specification, installation and in-use performance
look like, which is the benchmark an inspection is implicitly measured against
when a finding is challenged.

The practical consequence is a currency question rather than a technical one:
anyone quoting BS 8214 in a specification, an inspection report or a remedial
schedule needs to be clear which edition they are working from, because a
reference to "BS 8214" without a year now spans two documents a decade apart.

We describe the fire door inspection process itself in our
[guide to fire door inspections](/guides/fire-door-inspections-explained), which
does not depend on the edition.
