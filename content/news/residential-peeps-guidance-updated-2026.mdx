The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government updated *Residential
PEEPs: Guidance for Responsible Persons* on 6 April 2026 — the day the
<LegislationLink slug="fire-safety-residential-evacuation-plans-england-regulations-2025">Fire Safety (Residential Evacuation Plans) (England) Regulations 2025</LegislationLink>
came into force. The guidance was first published on 2 December 2024.

## What it asks responsible persons to do

The guidance sets out a sequence rather than a checklist, and the order matters.

Identify residents whose ability to evacuate would be compromised. Offer
person-centred assessments to discuss their specific needs. Put proportionate
mitigations in place based on what those assessments find. Record a written
emergency evacuation statement for each relevant resident. Share flat locations
and basic information about the degree of assistance needed with the fire and
rescue authority, with the resident's consent. Prepare and maintain a
building-wide evacuation plan as a separate document. Then review annually, or
sooner when circumstances change.

## The two parts that are harder than they look

**Consent.** Information-sharing with the fire and rescue authority is conditioned
on it. A resident who declines does not thereby remove the duty to assess and
mitigate — it removes one route by which the mitigation would otherwise have
worked, and the plan has to account for that rather than treat the refusal as an
end to the matter.

**"Relevant resident" is not a fixed list.** The guidance expects annual review or
sooner on change, and in a block with normal turnover the population it describes
changes continuously. A one-off exercise completed in April will be inaccurate by
autumn unless something in the letting or housing-management process keeps it
current.

## How this sits with the fire risk assessment

The building evacuation plan and the fire risk assessment are separate documents
doing different jobs, and the guidance keeps them separate. The assessment
establishes whether the building's fire protection supports its evacuation
strategy at all; the evacuation plan sets out how that strategy is executed for
the people actually living there. A plan built on a strategy the building cannot
support is the failure this pairing is designed to surface.
