The Health and Safety Executive began a round of asbestos management inspections
on 1 April 2026, marked to Global Asbestos Awareness Week, with compliance checks
continuing through the year.

## Who is being visited

Building owners, landlords and tradespeople, across offices, factories and shops
as well as schools, hospitals and places of worship. The common factor is a
building old enough to contain asbestos and a duty holder responsible for managing
it, not a particular sector.

## The three failures inspectors are finding

HSE has named what it is looking for, which is more useful than a general warning:

Asbestos management plans not in place to ensure the condition of
asbestos-containing materials is routinely monitored. Information, instruction and
training for managing asbestos that is inadequate. And procedures and arrangements
for work liable to disturb known or presumed asbestos-containing materials that
need improving.

## What the duty actually requires

Arrange a survey. Create a register and assess the risk. Write a management plan.
Then put the plan into action and keep it under review — which is the step that
separates a compliant building from one with a survey in a drawer.

The last of those is where our own experience matches HSE's finding. The plan is
usually the part that exists; the monitoring, the training and the arrangements for
contractors who might disturb material are the parts that quietly lapse between
building changes.

Non-compliance can lead to prosecution, fines and a criminal record. The
<NewsLink slug="asbestos-demolition-prosecution-march-2026">Cannock demolition case sentenced the month before this campaign opened</NewsLink>
shows what that looks like in practice, including for named individuals.
