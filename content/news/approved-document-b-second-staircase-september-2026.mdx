Amended guidance in
<StandardLink slug="approved-document-b-fire-safety">Approved Document B</StandardLink>
Volume 1 recommending a second staircase in blocks of flats with a storey 18 m or
more in height takes effect on 30 September 2026. MHCLG confirmed the threshold
and the date in circular 04/2024.

## The transitional provisions are the operative detail

The 2019 edition, incorporating the 2020, 2022 and 2025 amendment sets, continues
to apply where a building notice, initial notice or building control approval
application was submitted before 30 September 2026 **and** the work is
sufficiently progressed before that date or within eighteen months afterwards —
giving a longstop of 30 March 2028.

Both limbs have to be satisfied. A notice submitted in good time on a scheme that
then stalls does not preserve the earlier position indefinitely, and that is the
detail most likely to catch a project out.

## Guidance, not a regulation

Worth stating plainly, because this provision is routinely described as though the
Building Regulations had been amended. They have not. The Building Regulations set
functional requirements; Approved Document B describes a way of meeting them. The
second staircase recommendation is guidance on satisfying an existing requirement,
not a new requirement in its own right — which is why a departure remains possible
where it can be justified, and why "we are not following ADB" is a position that
has to be demonstrated rather than simply asserted.

## How it relates to the consultation

This is a separate and already-settled change. The
<NewsLink slug="approved-document-b-review-consultation-2026">consultation that closed on 1 July 2026</NewsLink>
proposed a further and wider set of amendments, including evacuation lifts above
18 m. The second staircase date below is fixed; that consultation's outcome is
not yet.

## For existing buildings

Nothing here retrospectively changes an occupied building. What it changes is the
benchmark for new work, and therefore what a building completed after this date
should have been given — which is where the original expectation comes from when
an assessment finds a protected route compromised years later.
