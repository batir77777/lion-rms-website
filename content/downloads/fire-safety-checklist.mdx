A practical starting point for landlords and responsible persons. This is a
self-check to help you spot obvious issues before a professional assessment.

## What this checklist is not

It is a **general template** and it must be adapted to your premises. It does
not replace a fire risk assessment, which is a legal requirement under the
<LegislationLink slug="regulatory-reform-fire-safety-order-2005">Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005</LegislationLink>.

Article 9 of the Order requires the
<GlossaryLink slug="responsible-person">responsible person</GlossaryLink> to make
a *suitable and sufficient* assessment of the risks, and to have it carried out
by someone <GlossaryLink slug="competent-person">competent</GlossaryLink> to do
it. Working through the questions below does not discharge that duty, however
carefully it is done. A completed checklist is a prompt for the conversation you
then have with an assessor — not a record that the assessment has happened.

Nothing here is premises-specific advice. Buildings differ, and the question
that matters in yours may not be on this list.

## Means of escape

- Escape routes are clear, unobstructed, and adequately wide.
- Final exit doors open easily from the inside without a key.
- Escape routes have emergency lighting where needed.
- Travel distances to a place of safety are reasonable.

## Fire doors and compartmentation

- <GlossaryLink slug="fire-door">Fire doors</GlossaryLink> are fitted with
  intumescent strips and, where required, cold smoke seals.
- Fire doors close fully onto the frame and are not wedged open.
- Gaps around fire doors are within tolerance (around 3&nbsp;mm).
- Penetrations through walls and floors for services and cables are properly
  fire-stopped.

## Detection and warning

- Smoke or heat detection is provided appropriate to the building.
- The fire alarm, if fitted, is tested regularly and records are kept.
- Occupants know how the alarm sounds and what to do.

## Firefighting and signage

- Appropriate fire extinguishers are provided, serviced and accessible.
- Fire action notices and escape signage are displayed and legible.
- Emergency lighting is tested and maintained.

## Management and records

- A current <GlossaryLink slug="fire-risk-assessment">fire risk assessment</GlossaryLink>
  is in place and acted upon.
- Remedial actions have owners and target dates.
- Testing, servicing and training records are kept up to date.
- Occupants and residents have clear fire safety information.

## If something here is not right

A checklist tells you that a question is worth asking; it does not tell you how
serious the answer is. A wedged-open fire door in a single-storey office and the
same door on the only escape stair of a residential block are not the same
finding, and the difference is exactly the judgement an assessment exists to
make.
