A twelve-month record for the routine monthly function test of
<GlossaryLink slug="emergency-lighting">emergency lighting</GlossaryLink>, with a
separate record of the annual full-duration test, a luminaire schedule and a
defects and actions log.

## What this record is for

To keep the monthly checks, the annual test, the luminaire references, the
failures and the repairs in one place, so that a defect can be followed from the
month it was found to the day it was put right.

It keeps two different activities apart, because they are done by different
people. The monthly functional check is a walk round the escape routes with a
test key, and the person doing it can record it here. The annual full-duration
test is not that: it is arranged and carried out under your competent
maintenance arrangements, in accordance with the system design and the
manufacturer's instructions. This form records that it happened and what it
achieved. **It does not tell anyone how to do it, and it is not authority for an
untrained person to run it.**

The annual sheet asks for the duration actually achieved rather than "pass". A
system that managed forty minutes of a three-hour duration has not passed, and a
system whose achieved duration falls year on year is saying something worth
hearing early.

## Who it is for

<GlossaryLink slug="responsible-person">Responsible persons</GlossaryLink> under
the <LegislationLink slug="regulatory-reform-fire-safety-order-2005">Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005</LegislationLink>
and the people who carry out the routine checks on their behalf: facilities
managers, caretakers, building managers and maintenance staff in offices,
schools, care settings, retail units and the common parts of residential blocks.

It is designed for someone walking the escape routes with a test key, not for a
lighting engineer.

## What it does not do

This is a **general template**. It records that a test happened. It does not tell
you whether your emergency lighting design is adequate for your building,
whether the coverage is right, or how long the system needs to run — those come
from the system design and from your
<GlossaryLink slug="fire-risk-assessment">fire risk assessment</GlossaryLink>.

It does not replace, and must not be presented as:

- a competent fire risk assessment;
- professional fire-engineering advice;
- a fire door survey;
- statutory inspection, testing, servicing or maintenance by a
  <GlossaryLink slug="competent-person">competent person</GlossaryLink>;
- premises-specific legal advice.

One point carried on the form itself, not only here: **a full-duration test
leaves the batteries discharged, and the system will not provide its rated
duration again until they have recharged.** It therefore belongs at a time when
the premises can tolerate reduced emergency lighting cover — not the start of a
normal occupied day — and the building should not be left in normal occupation
relying on a system that has just been fully discharged.

No servicing, testing or inspection interval is stated anywhere in this
resource, and neither is any recharge period. The monthly rhythm it is laid out
around is the premises' own arrangement, not an instruction from us.

## Available formats

The PDF is the one to print. It is A4 portrait throughout, six pages, with the
monthly grid on one sheet, the annual test blocks on another, then the luminaire
schedule and the defects log.

The Word file is the same document, editable, for anyone who needs to adapt the
premises details or the column headings before printing.

The spreadsheet carries the same content for those keeping digital records, on
four sheets — monthly check, annual test, luminaires and defects — with the
header rows frozen, drop-down lists on the yes/no columns, and the achieved
duration held as text so that spreadsheet time arithmetic cannot quietly change
what was written down.

Complete the luminaire schedule first. Everything else refers to it: without
references, "luminaire 14 failed" means nothing to the next person.

## Accessibility

The PDF is tagged, with a document title, en-GB language metadata, a real
heading structure and table headers that repeat across page breaks. It is
portrait throughout, so there is a single reading order from the first page to
the last, and the annual test section is a set of labelled blocks with its own
caption rather than a continuation of the monthly grid. Yes and no answers are
identified by text, never by colour or shading alone. The Word file carries the
same title, language and heading structure. The spreadsheet has named
worksheets, a frozen header row that is also set as the print title, and no
merged cells in any data range.

There is no printable HTML version of this resource, and that is deliberate.
Nobody reads this document; they fill it in, month after month, and keep it. The
artefact is a ruled grid that lives in a logbook or a folder, and an HTML
equivalent would be a worse version of the thing people actually want. The
explanatory content lives on this page instead.

## Disclaimer

This form is intended solely as a record of routine emergency lighting testing.
It is not a fire risk assessment, does not certify compliance, and must not be
relied upon to determine whether the emergency lighting installation is adequate
for the premises. It cannot confirm system design, coverage, illuminance levels,
rated duration, battery condition or installation quality. Completing it does
not by itself discharge any legal duty. Where defects are identified or
compliance is uncertain, advice should be obtained from a suitably competent
person.
